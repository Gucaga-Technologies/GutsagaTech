A. FIVE NEW KNOWLEDGE ARTICLES, BUILT FROM THE FOUNDER'S BOOK
=============================================================
The Knowledge Hub had eight thin pages (~450 words each). It now also carries a
series of five long articles, in English, taken from the operational chapters of
"The Evolution of the Pharmaceutical Market". 8,400 words in total.

They are written for pharmacy chains and pharma distribution, and they follow
the book's own staging — one chain growing up, step by step:

  1. knowledge/availability-as-a-financial-metric.html      (1,600 words)
     "Availability is a financial number, not a shelf number"
     What an empty shelf really costs; why 5 points of availability can mean a
     ~20% difference in sales; how to define availability through lost margin
     (potential margin = actual + estimated lost margin); why an ERP cannot tell
     you what availability was 90 days ago; the four ways to break the number
     down (supplier, category manager, pharmacy, time); the bonus trap.

  2. knowledge/cost-of-high-availability.html               (1,545 words)
     "What high availability costs"
     Days of stock and why it is meaningless without availability beside it;
     where a growing chain's money is lying; how supplier terms can finance
     growth; using turnover to find the manufacturer, buyer or pharmacy where
     capital is stuck; the owner who called to ask what to do with the cash.

  3. knowledge/one-policy-hundred-pharmacies.html           (1,824 words)
     "Can a hundred pharmacies manage stock the same way?"
     One policy versus a hundred daily decisions; why an owner's instruction
     does not change the system; the five levels of ordering maturity; ordering
     driven by forecast versus by the state of the stock; the chain of 350
     pharmacies where the twenty that objected were running the same
     availability on ~50 days of stock against the network's ~35.

  4. knowledge/central-warehouse-less-stock.html            (1,892 words)
     "Why adding a warehouse can reduce total stock"
     The aggregation paradox, worked through: 100 pharmacies × 2 packs = 200,
     against ~129-143 with a central warehouse at the same availability. With a
     new diagram. Protection period, the overnight replenishment cycle, pooled
     uncertainty, the ~30% two projects actually saw, breaking bulk, pharmacist
     time, resilience during a market shortage, buying power.

  5. knowledge/cheaper-purchase-price.html                  (1,531 words)
     "Does buying cheaper always mean earning more?"
     Direct purchasing and private label; the distributor-vs-manufacturer
     comparison; GMROI and its bands; "how many months of demand are in one
     compulsory lot?" (24,000 ÷ 1,000 = 24 months); parallel trade caveats;
     seven questions before signing a direct contract.

B. FIXES AND CHANGES FROM YOUR REVIEW
======================================

1. VALUE CALCULATOR — FITS, AND ASKS FOR NUMBERS PEOPLE KNOW
   The results table was set to "never wrap" inside a box set to "hide what
   sticks out", so a long number (~$1.7M freed) was chopped off at the edge.
   Fixed proportions and wrapping cells now, the section is wider, type steps
   down on smaller screens. Checked at 1440 / 1200 / 768 / 390 px with eight
   sets of numbers, from a 5-shop chain to an $800M distributor: no clipping.

   "Inventory turns, days (ITR)" is gone — most people do not know it. The
   calculator asks for Sales / year (all locations) and derives the stock cover
   itself, showing it back: "From your sales, margin and stock: 65 days of stock
   (5.6 turns a year)", with a warning when the numbers cannot both be true.
   "Reorder every ... days" is now "Replenishment time, days".

2. THE ORDERING-LABOUR AMBIGUITY — CONFIRMED AND FIXED
   That review was right, and it mattered most for distributors. Hours per day
   were multiplied by the NUMBER OF LOCATIONS, so read as "hours per person" a
   100-location chain was overstated 100x and a 3-warehouse distributor was
   understated. The field is now "Ordering hours / person / day" and the saving
   is computed per person against an 8-hour day, using the headcount already
   being asked for. Default 5 h/person/day.

3. OTHER CALCULATOR ERRORS FOUND WHILE CHECKING (none reported, all fixed)
   - Margin was applied as mark-up on cost in one place and margin on sales in
     another; it is now consistently gross margin % of sales.
   - Empty or zero fields silently kept the previous value; they now fall back
     to the example value, and every field is bounded.
   - Impossible sales/stock combinations freed more cash than the company owned.
   - A business already lean was still promised a 20% stock cut because its fill
     rate was high; it now gets the small, honest number.
   - Stock reduction capped at 40%, sales uplift at 20% — as published elsewhere.
   - Values above a billion no longer print as "$999929.4B".

4. HOMEPAGE -> BENEFITS DID NOTHING
   The "Benefits" menu item pointed at "index.html" — from the homepage that IS
   the homepage. Same bug on all 9 Knowledge pages, where it landed on the
   Knowledge index. Fixed on all 10, then every menu item on every page was
   checked against the page it names: 0 mismatches left.

   - Technologies hero: the laptop-and-dashboards render from the homepage,
