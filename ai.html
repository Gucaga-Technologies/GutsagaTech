Company and history

Gutsaga Technologies is a Lithuanian inventory-technology company building Horizon, a management-control platform for inventory flow in retail chains, distributors and wholesalers. The company's inventory-management work goes back to 2004. Since 2005, founder Serzas Gutsaga worked within the Theory of Constraints / Inherent Simplicity / Symphony implementation ecosystem, including historical working visits at Goldratt House in Israel and direct work with Amir and Eli Schragenheim. The team applied TOC, Dynamic Buffer Management, Make-to-Availability and production/distribution logic in real implementations before creating its own technology.

Horizon is positioned as the next step in that line of development: GT kept the flow and buffer-management principles that worked, then developed additional control mechanisms where real implementations exposed limits — including policy automation, small-target logic, more detailed overstock zones and decisions, redistribution, expiration control, promotions and management visibility. GT technologies have been used across 10,000+ stores, pharmacies, points of sale and warehouses.

What Horizon does

Horizon is not a forecasting tool and not a black box. It continuously measures how adequate the stock of every product in every location is, and adjusts targets, orders and transfers the moment reality moves — under explicit ordering rules that managers see and can change. Every SKU-location is classified into zones (from unwanted overstock through the OK zone to safety stock), and each zone comes with a concrete action: order, expedite, transfer, promote, stop buying or clear out.

Measured results

  • Product availability (fill rate): up to 99.5%
  • Inventory reduction: typically 10-40% without losing sales (20% is already very good)
  • Sales growth from availability: 2-20% (a 1pp availability gain gives roughly 2%+ sales; 5pp roughly 20%)
  • Manual ordering time: down up to 85%; two people can run ordering for 300 locations
  • Unique capability: full day-by-day history of lost sales and overstock at every level (store, warehouse, supplier, category, SKU) — almost no other system keeps this

Key concepts in one paragraph

Replenishment time (order frequency + production + delivery until sellable) drives how much stock a location needs: enough to cover maximum regular consumption during that window. Shortening it reduces required inventory. Aggregation means demand fluctuates less the further upstream you look, so central stock plus fast replenishment beats pushing inventory into stores. Replenishment is pull-based: each layer orders only what restores its own target. Priority between competing needs is set by target penetration — the share of the target that is missing. Around the target, stock zones (unwanted/tolerated/allowed overstock, OK zone, low inventory, urgent, ground, safety stock) attach a concrete action to every position: order, expedite, transfer, promote, reprice or clear out.
